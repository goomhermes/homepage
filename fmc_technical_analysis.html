1. FMC Technical Analysis — September 15, 2026

Trend: Very Bearish

The recent sequence is:

  • Sept. 2: $13.33, after a large +12.2% day
  • Sept. 3–11: eight straight sessions without a meaningful recovery
  • Sept. 11: $11.40, −5.63%
  • Sept. 14: $10.98, −3.68%

That places the stock about 17.6% below the Sept. 2 close in only eight trading sessions.

Lower high → lower low → lower high → lower low

There is currently no technical evidence of a confirmed reversal.

3. Support Levels

$10.65–10.80 — Immediate Support

FMC traded around $10.65–10.78 on Aug. 27–28 before recovering sharply toward $13.45. This creates a significant short-term demand zone around $10.60–10.80.

$9.95–10.00 — Major Support

This is the 52-week low and therefore the most important established technical support.

If $10 holds and price stabilizes, a double-bottom-type structure could potentially develop. A decisive break below $9.95 would materially worsen the technical picture because there is little established support immediately underneath.

6. Volume

This is one of the more reassuring aspects of the recent action.

  • Sept. 11 decline: approximately 3.0M shares, versus a 50-day average around 3.6M.
  • Sept. 14: approximately 3M shares.
  • Sept. 2: approximately 6.35M shares during a +12.2% advance.

Thus the recent decline has not occurred on extraordinary capitulation volume. It looks more like persistent distribution and an absence of buyers than a single massive institutional liquidation event.

7. Technical Scenarios

Bear Case — Break $10.60

$10.60 → $9.95

If $10.60 fails, a test of the $9.95 52-week low becomes likely. A clean break below $9.95 would be a major technical warning.

Neutral / Bottoming Case

$10.60–11.00 → $11.40 → $12.00

The first credible indication of stabilization would be a sustained move back above $11.40. A close above that level followed by a successful retest would be more meaningful than merely touching it.

Bullish Reversal Case

$10.6–11.0 holds → $11.40 → $12.00 → $12.40–12.50 → $13.30+

Only after reclaiming approximately $12.40–12.50 would the chart begin to look substantially healthier. A break above $13.30 would invalidate the current bearish short-term structure.

CTVA — Best FMC Comparison

Corteva is probably the most informative peer because both companies have substantial crop-protection exposure.

If CTVA begins breaking down sharply, that would suggest FMC's weakness is increasingly sector-wide. If CTVA stabilizes or rallies while FMC continues making new lows, that points more strongly toward FMC-specific problems.

CF — Important Macro Confirmation

CF has been technically much stronger, trading near its 52-week high. This suggests that agricultural-input stocks as a whole are not collapsing.

MOS — Useful but Less Direct

Mosaic is more exposed to fertilizer than crop protection. It has been considerably weaker than CF but nowhere near as extreme as FMC.

XLB — Broad Materials-Sector Reference

The Materials Select Sector SPDR ETF (XLB) is useful as a broad market reference, but it is not an ideal primary FMC indicator because FMC's collapse appears considerably more company- and industry-specific than a normal materials-sector move.

10. What to Watch

The most useful hierarchy is:

FMC → CTVA → MOS / NTR / CF → XLB → S&P 500

The current relative-strength picture is approximately:

CF strong → CTVA relatively strong → MOS weak → FMC extremely weak

This suggests FMC currently carries a large company-specific discount, rather than simply tracking the agricultural sector.

FMC's potential strategic-sale situation and its unusually high short interest also create the possibility of a sharp short-covering rebound if a credible catalyst appears.

Relative-strength signal to monitor

If FMC begins outperforming CTVA while remaining below $12, that could be an early relative-strength signal before the absolute price chart turns bullish.

If CTVA remains stable or strengthens while FMC continues making new lows, the divergence would increasingly argue for FMC-specific weakness.
